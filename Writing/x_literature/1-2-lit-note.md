# Goal:
+ To estimate the GEP for water provisioning services for irrigation, municipal, and industrial use:
  + How much natural water (raw water) contributes to the well-being (in monetary value) of each sector across the world?
  + How much raw water (natural water) produced by water provisioning ecosystem service contributes to the economic output of each sector across the world?

+ What is the value of water?
  + "value of water = the benefits derived from access, use or consumption of a volume of water or a water body" (@Grafton.etal2023)
  + "The value of water is the benefit water consumers derive from access use, or consumption of water, such as drinking, food production, or cultural purposes (United Nations, 2021)." (@Grafton.etal2023)

# Questions:
+ As a part or the Global GEP project, we are interested in estimating the GEP for water provisioning services for irrigation, municipal, and industrial use. For example, to estimate the GEP for water provisioning services for municipal use, we want to ask "How much of the municipal water production is attributable to raw water produced by nature?".
  + In principle, resource rent approach can be used to estimate such value. But in practice, it does not work because water tariffs often do not reflect the value of raw water itself.
  + So, the second best approach, which is often used in the literature (e.g., @Vardon.etal2019; @Edens.Graveland2014), is the replacement cost method. This method estimates the least cost of replacing the benefits of ES with alternatives. Specifically, it asks the question "How much would it cost to replace the benefits of water provisioning services with alternatives?". 
  + To me, this question is a bit different from the question of "How much of the production value of municipal water comes from nature?" because the replacement cost method does not consider how valuable the raw water is to producing municipal water.

+ **Water provisioning services**:
  + SEEA-EEA, European Union, European Environment Agency: "water provisioning is defined as 'surface water used for nutrition, materials or energy and; groundwater used for nutirition, materials or energy'" (@Vardon.etal2019)


+ The value of water provisioning services should be vary depending on the use ... (@Grafton.etal2020)

+ Conceptually, the value of water itself can be recovered as a resource rent. Suppose that the water tariff contains the value of water itself, then the resource rent of water can be calculated as the difference between the total revenue from water use and the total cost of water use (excluding the cost of water itself). However, in practice, this approach is not valid as the water tariff often does not reflect the value of water itself, and it is difficult to separate the cost of water itself from the total cost of water use (@Vardon.etal2019).
